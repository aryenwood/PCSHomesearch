Your VA loan, step by step.

For anyone who qualifies: active duty and veterans of every branch, Guard and Reserve members, and some surviving spouses.

  1. Get your Certificate of Eligibility (COE)

    Your lender can request it online, or you can at va.gov. What you'll need:

    • Veteran: your DD214.
    • Active duty: a statement of service signed by your commander, adjutant or personnel officer.
    • Guard or Reserve: your DD214 if you were activated; otherwise a signed statement of service, NGB Form 22 and NGB Form 23, or your latest retirement points statement.
    • Surviving spouse: VA Form 26-1817 if you receive DIC; otherwise VA Form 21P-534EZ, your marriage license and the death certificate.
    Request your COE at va.gov →

  2. Get pre-approved

    The VA sets no minimum credit score; lenders set their own. The VA's guideline is monthly debts at 41% of income or less. BAH and BAS count as income. The VA also checks what's left each month: in the Northeast, a family of four with a loan of $80,000 or more needs $1,025.

  3. Know the funding fee

    Down paymentFirst VA loanUsed one before
    Less than 5%2.15%3.3%
    5% or more1.5%1.5%
    10% or more1.25%1.25%

    On a $300,000 loan with nothing down, that's $6,450 the first time and $9,900 after. No fee if you get VA disability pay (or qualify for it but take retirement or active-duty pay instead), are rated before closing, receive DIC, or are active duty with a Purple Heart.

  4. Know your loan limit

    With your full entitlement, the VA sets no limit; your lender decides what you qualify for. If part of it is still tied up in an earlier VA loan, the 2026 county limit in Jefferson, Lewis and St. Lawrence counties is $832,750.

  5. Know the costs

    No down payment and no mortgage insurance with full entitlement, if the price isn't above the appraised value. The lender's own charge is capped at 1%. The seller can pay up to 4% in concessions. New York mortgage recording tax totals $0.75 per $100 in Jefferson and St. Lawrence counties and $1.00 in Lewis; ask your lender or closing attorney how much of it you pay. New York closings use attorneys. Paying your own buyer's agent is allowed in some cases; ask your lender.

  6. Older and rural homes

    A well must be tested by a third party, not you, and results are good for 90 days. Peeling paint on a home built before 1978 has to be fixed. Heat must be permanent and keep the house at 50 degrees. A septic check happens only if the appraiser sees a problem, and no termite inspection is required in these three counties unless there's damage. Get your own home inspection: the appraisal isn't one.

  7. Moving in

    You certify you'll live there within 60 days. If you're deployed or on active duty, your spouse living there counts.

  8. Property taxes

    New York's veterans exemptions are local options: your county, town and school district each decide whether to offer them. File form RP-458-a with your town assessor by March 1 in most towns, and again after you buy a new home.

Know what you're owed (2026).

This moving pay comes with PCS orders if the numbers are right and the paperwork is filed on time. You can verify everything with your finance office.

Moving allowance (DLA)

A one-time payment toward the costs of setting up a new home, like deposits and utility hookups. The amount depends on rank and whether you have dependents. It isn't automatic, so it helps to ask your finance office or request it in the DFAS SmartVoucher app; up to 80% can come as an advance.

$1,870 to $6,385One time

Mileage for driving your car (MALT)

Paid for each car you're authorized to drive, based on the official distance between duty stations. This rate took effect July 1, 2026.

$0.235Per mile

Daily pay on the drive (per diem)

$113 for lodging plus $68 for meals for the service member, each travel day, at the rate in effect from October 1, 2026. Family members 12 and older get 75% of that; children under 12 get 50%.

$181Per day, member

Hotel while you find a home (TLE)

Up to 21 days on a move within the U.S., used at your old duty station, your new one, or both. Up to 60 days in areas the DoD has approved for an extension. You'll want to keep every lodging receipt.

$290Max per day

Moving a pet

For one cat or dog on a move within the U.S. Keep the receipts.

$550Max, one pet

Child care while you wait

If no child care is available within 30 days of the date you need it at Fort Drum, a pilot program through September 30, 2027 pays toward the travel of a person who comes to care for your children. Request care at MilitaryChildCare.com before you move.

$500Max, in the U.S.

Money deadlines worth knowing.

01

The travel voucher is due within 5 working days of arriving at Fort Drum. That's the DoD rule, and filing it is how you get paid for the trip.

02

Movers damaged your home, or an essential item (a refrigerator, mattress, washer and dryer, or medical equipment) is lost or broken: tell the moving company within 7 days. For anything else lost or damaged, give notice in DPS within 180 days of delivery, then file the claim within 12 months of delivery (9 months if your shipment was picked up before May 15, 2026). Note visible damage on the inventory before you sign. Filing late means you only get the depreciated value.

03

If you can, photograph belongings and serial numbers before pack day. It's your evidence for claims.

04

If you're moving some of it yourself (a PPM), you'll need certified weight tickets, empty and loaded. You're paid up to what the move would have cost the government, within your weight allowance, so it helps to get a cost estimate from your Transportation Office before you commit. Submit your PPM claim within 45 days of finishing the move.

Movers won't pack these.

  • Cleaners with bleach or ammonia, paint, gasoline & other hazmat
  • Propane tanks (even small camping ones), ammunition, fireworks
  • Plants, perishable food, and open containers
  • Medications, important documents, and jewelry: keep these with you in an essentials bag.
